This is an affordable mini CNC mill built from Ender 3 parts. It’s suitable for tasks such as milling, engraving, drawing, and PCB fabrication. The machine is driven by NEMA 17 motors, belts, and two lead screws. It uses an old Dremel that I had lying around as a spindle, and I'm currently working on making an enclosure out of plywood and an acrylic front door. It use klipper software to run, with a "Klipperized" post-processor to make work on Fusion 360 CAM. Credit to @IMLAB, @SGD_Knuffel, and the team at Ender CNC for providing most of the printables. I used a mix of those files to create an Ender-3 CNC that I felt was most optimized. I made this so I can level up my future projects with acrylic and aluminum parts without having to pay a bunch to get them milled. I also hope to continue working on improving those files in the coming weeks and possibly even mill the sides out of aluminum. Other than using those files, I did not follow any specific guide and did everything else myself. 


Wiring Diagram 



![Wiring](https://github.com/dillpickckle/ENDER-3-CNC/blob/main/Images/Wiring.png)

As you can see its pretty simple. Just connect the xyz ports to their respective motors and do the same with the endstops. Also make sure to connect the board to the ender power supply.
